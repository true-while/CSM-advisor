# Route, Ring and Journey Decisions

## Decision order

Use the same ordered reasoning for each cohort. These are transparent recommendation rules, not an automated eligibility score.

1. Check permitted purpose, privacy, customer/program eligibility and a plausible skilling need. If the principal problem is a service incident, missing feature, access failure or unresolved policy decision, pause the affected learning activity and identify its owner.
2. Establish the outcome and the cohort's current behavior. Separate observed facts from hypotheses. Missing evidence yields a provisional route or an intake-only draft.
3. If deterioration or confidence/governance barriers are evidenced and learning can address them, consider Protect first for the affected cohort. Diagnose the cause; do not infer it from renewal urgency alone.
4. Otherwise, if first useful use has not been established, select Activate. If recurring use exists but reliable workflow performance is lacking, select Deepen.
5. Select Extend only for a transfer initiative supported by an accepted prior value review, a safe reusable workflow artifact, a legitimate receiving need and owned readiness. A receiving cohort without basics may run Activate within the wider extension initiative. Do not label its readiness proven because the originating cohort succeeded.
6. If more than one condition fits, split materially different cohorts. For a single cohort, prefer its immediate addressable barrier and state the rejected alternative. If evidence is insufficient to resolve the choice, show conditional candidates, not a forced result.
7. Prioritize one of the two learning-path lenses below from the addressable skilling need, independently of route and package. With missing evidence, retain conditional candidates and identify the smallest task diagnostic needed.
8. Check delivery eligibility, official duration, timing, budget and capacity as separate feasibility gates. A route and learning path can be appropriate while a delivery remains blocked.

No route progression is mandatory. No route selection approves delivery. Do not introduce a fifth commercial route.

## Route decision table

| Route | Positive evidence | Package candidates | Exit or transfer evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Activate | Relevant access exists or has an owned readiness plan; useful first use is absent or unreliable; a practical initial task is agreed | A1 for one bounded first-use task; A2 for a pilot-to-rollout readiness decision | Demonstrated safe repeatable first use enables Deepen; unresolved access pauses affected practice. |
| Deepen | Recurring appropriate use; a specific role or technical task falls below an agreed rubric | D1 for workflow proficiency; D2 for technical learning and operational handoff | Task proficiency plus business evidence may support Extend; a new technical defect goes to support. |
| Protect | Comparable deterioration or dated recurring friction; an addressable learning or confidence component has been established | P1 for recovery; P2 for understanding already-authorized governance and safe-use boundaries | Restored access/trust and first use may return to Activate or Deepen; absence of an addressable skill gap ends or redesigns the intervention. |
| Extend | Sponsor-accepted prior value evidence and a legitimate new role/use case; receiving readiness and local ownership | E1 for one transferable workflow; E2 for a sustained advocate network | Receiving-cohort evidence supports further expansion, Deepen, Protect, pause or close. No automatic commercial conversion. |

Do not hard-code an adoption percentage or period as a live trigger. Use an owner-approved threshold with its denominator, comparable period, exclusions, sample adequacy and confounders. Without one, explain a qualitative signal and propose a baseline or diagnostic review. Never use a missing observation as zero.

## Two primary learning-path lenses

Select the primary lens for each coherent cohort from dated task evidence, observed practice or customer-confirmed needs. Prioritize the gap that prevents the intended outcome, not a universal order, a role title alone, visible training capacity or a product label. Learning suitability and delivery feasibility remain separate decisions.

| Learning-path lens | Prioritize when | Design and assessment | Illustrative label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feature-based or app-based | Confidence or proficiency in a particular app/capability is the immediate bottleneck | Focus on the relevant app task and assess safe, independent task completion against an agreed rubric. Verify the capability and environment before affected practice. | "Learn Copilot in Word" |
| Persona-based or role-based | Basic app fluency is sufficient, but applying Copilot across an agreed role workflow and outcome is the bottleneck | Organize learning around the role's end-to-end work. Embed only the app capabilities the workflow requires; assess the workflow and its output against an agreed role-task rubric. | "Copilot for Finance" |

These labels illustrate learning design, not official catalog offerings, exact course titles, license entitlement or product/environment availability. Persona refers only to job responsibilities and workflows, never protected traits. Do not infer a person's skill from their job title or demographic characteristics.

1. **Feature priority:** If task evidence identifies an app-fluency gap, select feature/app-based as primary and name the task and success evidence. Reconsider persona learning only if a role-application gap remains after the relevant fluency is demonstrated.
2. **Persona priority:** If basic fluency is evidenced and the gap is role application, select persona/role-based as primary. Do not repeat foundational app learning without a demonstrated prerequisite gap.
3. **Mixed needs:** If a small app gap prevents a role workflow, propose persona learning with a bounded feature prerequisite and a task-based readiness check. If the app gap dominates, select feature learning first with a conditional role-workflow transfer. Split materially different cohorts instead of requiring everyone to complete both paths. Mixed is a composition, not a third primary path. Deduplicate overlapping official content, participants and credits.
4. **Unknown or blocked:** If evidence cannot distinguish the bottleneck, show conditional candidates and a diagnostic task rather than inventing a primary path. Access, licensing, environment, governance or readiness gaps pause the affected activity. A provisional design can continue, but a different lens does not cure a non-skilling blocker.
5. **Record and review:** For each cohort, state primary path or conditional candidates, reason and evidence, audience, intended task/outcome, assessment, the alternative considered, transfer condition and applicable gates. Reassess the lens when task evidence changes. A lens change does not automatically change route, package, booking or budget.

The lenses fit within Activate, Deepen, Protect and Extend and the existing eight packages. Do not impose Feature = Activate or Persona = Deepen. An Activate task can be framed around a role when basic app fluency is already established; a recurring-use cohort in Deepen can still need focused feature proficiency. Package selection must independently satisfy its entry and outcome criteria. A subway-style journey may show the selected learning focus on a route and conditional transfers, never compulsory Feature-to-Persona stations or a fifth route.

## Ring roles and dependencies

| Ring | Distinct responsibilities | Evidence before the next handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaders and champions | Leaders set the outcome and remove organizational barriers. Champions are hands-on power users who test and demonstrate safe practice. | Sponsor agreement and an approved task; champion evidence of application, not attendance alone. |
| Technical leads | Validate access, supported environment, governance dependencies, lab readiness and support ownership. | Relevant prerequisites and permitted scenarios ready, or clearly owned gaps with affected activities paused. |
| Advocates | Reinforce approved practice, coach peers and direct issues to appropriate owners. | Approved resources, support boundaries, local capacity and a safe feedback channel. |
| End users | Apply the capability to real permitted tasks and contribute aggregate outcome evidence. | Suitable readiness, individual registration where required, practice time and an assessment method. |

Run sponsorship and technical readiness in parallel. Bring champions into bounded practice after the relevant safety prerequisites pass. Broaden with advocates available and evidence supporting the handoff. Keep sponsorship active throughout. Do not require leaders to attend an MTT class as a universal prerequisite.

Power user is a capability role, not a fifth ring. Ring participation may overlap. Unique reach is the union of approved participant identifiers, not the sum of ring totals. When only aggregate overlapping counts exist, report unique reach as unknown or as a validated source-provided figure.

## Package composition rules

Choose the minimum package set that closes the evidenced gap. State each package's cohort, role, outcome and dependency. Carry the selected learning-path lens into the objectives, practice and assessment of each package without changing its route or purpose. Avoid duplicating the same official learning across packages or the two learning-path lenses. Distinguish a shared customer-owned readiness meeting from an MTT delivery. Do not assume one attendee, one day or one approval can be counted independently for several overlapping lines.

Examples of conditional composition are A2 followed by D1 when the pilot reveals a proficiency gap; P2 before P1 when approved safe-use understanding must be restored before recovery practice; and E1 with an A1 receiving-cohort component when the new role lacks first use. These are illustrations, not compulsory bundles or catalog offerings.

Change customer terminology, approved examples, accessibility arrangements and practice sequence within the validated wrapper. Changes to official content, duration, format, labs, environment support or preparation require the applicable exception route and revalidation.

## Journey shapes

All durations below are design options from the operating plan, not official course durations or promises. Select a horizon from the customer's scope and current constraints, then rebuild it if mapping or lead time requires a different shape.

| Shape | Appropriate use | Non-negotiable distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-week QuickStart bridge | Customer/CSM-led task definition, sponsorship, access checks, approved asynchronous preparation and initial safe practice | It does not promise a newly requested MTT private delivery within two weeks. |
| Six-to-eight-week program | One bounded learning and practice cycle with evidence-led reinforcement | Request lead times occur before eligible instruction; preserve every official course duration. |
| Twelve-week worked pattern | A larger pilot and rollout with several conditional waves and handoffs | Wave weeks are placeholders; later-wave requests may be due before earlier waves complete. |
| Three-to-six-month campaign | Local advocate reinforcement and phased receiving cohorts tied to business milestones | Forecast demand separately from monthly approvals. Community meetings are not automatically MTT-funded days. |

### Relative twelve-week pattern, explicitly illustrative

Before week 1, clear relevant readiness and operational gates. Use weeks 1 to 2 for eligible foundational learning and champion practice; weeks 3 to 4 for validated role learning and a widen/repeat/pause decision; weeks 5 to 7 for ready end-user application; week 8 for justified reinforcement; weeks 9 to 12 for sustained practice and handoff. Rebuild this pattern around official course formats. Retain delivery-specific 60-day and 90-day reviews beyond the program when necessary. Historical dates, trainer-day totals and credits are not defaults. Unknown actual completion means relative review dates only.

## Prioritization and change decisions

When several eligible options exist, explain customer-outcome importance, real time sensitivity, addressable gap, readiness, sponsorship, learner burden and feasible delivery. Use qualitative comparisons with evidence, not an opaque score. Prefer customer impact and readiness to consuming visible capacity. Show the earliest feasible, balanced and contingency options only when evidence supports them; one honest option is better than three invented ones.

For a transfer, log prior route, proposed route, cohort, evidence IDs, reason, owner decision, effective date and measurement reset. Retain unsuccessful or inconclusive earlier outcomes. Continue, deepen, protect, extend, pause and close are review decisions; an entry decision may also select Activate. Neither continuing nor pausing automatically changes a booking or budget record.
